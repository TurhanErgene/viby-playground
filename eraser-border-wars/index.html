How the schoolyard played it

One eraser, red on one side, blue on the other. No dice, no board — just a map you drew yourselves.

01

Drag your finger across the rival tiles you want, starting on one that touches your border. One tap takes one tile. By eye — nobody measured anything.

02

The odds move with the bite. Hug your own border and keep it modest and the others believe you; a deep greedy spear into their middle is a flip you will usually lose. You see the numbers before you commit. This is the part the loudest kid used to shout about.

03

Flip the eraser. Red + and the land is yours. Blue − and you hand over the same amount, taken from the ground right behind your push, so a deep raid risks the shoulder you launched it from.

04

Land on a short end and the bet itself changes: ×2 doubles the bite, ÷2 halves it, and the eraser goes straight back up to settle the new one. Rarer than the rim, and it grows as the corners go.

05

Land on the narrow rim and you keep the land and draw a card. Arm one card before any flip: Double pays twice on a plus, Shield eats a minus, Re-flip buys a second throw, Judge talks the odds up. A card sits in your hand until it actually does something.

06

The bell ends recess after 12 rounds and whoever holds the most map wins. Squeezed under 8% and you give the rest up to whoever pushed you there, so nobody gets ground down tile by tile.

What's on the ground

△

Hills. Rising ground is hard to argue your way onto. Every hill tile in your bite costs you believability.

≈

The river. It runs along the tile edges, not through them. Reaching across it to take ground costs you more than walking around, except at the two fords, marked with planks. In the rain the fords are the only way over at all.

☁

The seasons. Recess runs through a year. Spring brings rain, summer a dry spell that lets you walk the riverbed, autumn a wind that doubles the rim, winter a fog that hides the odds until the eraser lands. Each round announces itself.

⚑

Capitals. Each flag sits on a real tile. The ground immediately around it is loose, because the crowd is all inside the walls, so the outskirts come away easily. The flag tile itself is the hard part, and taking it breaks the line: a further 30% of that player's land comes with it, and they plant a new flag in whatever is left.

◌

Old ground shows. Tiles that have changed hands keep a smudge of whoever held them before, so by the last rounds you can see where the fighting was.

◍

The eraser wears. Every throw rounds the corners a little more, so the rim comes up more often as recess goes on. Late flips are luckier than early ones.
